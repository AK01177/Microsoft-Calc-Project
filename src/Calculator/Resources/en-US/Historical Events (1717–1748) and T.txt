Historical Events (1717–1748) and Their Connections to Lecture-Based Readings
1. Decline of the Mughal Empire (1717–1748)
Themes:
Political fragmentation, rise of regional powers, military decline, social-cultural disruption.

Relevant Lecture-Based Readings:
Barbara D. & Thomas R. Metcalf – Foundation of Colonial Rule, Conquest and Settlement
(Direct Match): The weakening of central Mughal authority created opportunities for European powers to establish colonial rule.

Abhijit Banerjee & Lakshmi Iyer – Land Tenure Systems
(Theme Match): The jagirdari system's breakdown laid the foundation for colonial land and revenue systems.

Bernard S. Cohn – Study of Indian Society and Caste System by Europeans
(Theme Match): As Mughal power declined, Europeans began systematically studying Indian institutions like caste to understand and control society.

Diane P. Mines & Sarah Lamb – Seven Misconceptions about India’s Caste System
(Theme Match): The social disorder during Mughal decline reshaped caste and social mobility in ways later misunderstood.

Benedict Anderson – Print Capitalism and National Consciousness
(Theme Match): The regional fragmentation during this era contributed to linguistic and cultural identities, paving the way for future nationalism.

2. Rise of Regional Powers
Themes:
Decentralization of power, autonomy of regional elites, formation of new political and cultural identities.

Relevant Lecture-Based Readings:
Barbara D. & Thomas R. Metcalf – Foundation of Colonial Rule
(Direct Match): The emergence of states like Hyderabad, Maratha, and Bengal created a fragmented landscape that colonial powers exploited.

Abhijit Banerjee & Lakshmi Iyer – Land Tenure Systems
(Theme Match): Regional leaders introduced new land and administrative systems that would later interact with British policies.

Bernard S. Cohn – European Study of Indian Society
(Theme Match): The regionalization of authority complicated British ethnographic and administrative projects.

Benedict Anderson – National Consciousness
(Theme Match): Regional powers laid the cultural and linguistic groundwork for emergent forms of national identity.

3. First Carnatic War (1744–1748)
Themes:
European rivalry, military modernization, diplomacy, transformation of Indian geopolitics.

Relevant Lecture-Based Readings:
Barbara D. & Thomas R. Metcalf – Conquest and Settlement
(Direct Match): This war marks the first military confrontation between colonial powers in India, altering local power dynamics.

Milton Singer – Sanskritization and Modernization
(Theme Match): The colonial presence in Madras initiated institutional and urban modernization, later discussed by Singer.

Abhijit Banerjee & Lakshmi Iyer – Land Tenure Systems
(Theme Match): Military victories enabled new administrative models, influencing land revenue policies.

Benedict Anderson – Print/Vernacular/Nationalism
(Theme Match): Power shifts began shaping collective consciousness of foreign domination.

Sudipta Kaviraj / Niraja Gopal Jayal & Pratap Bhanu Mehta – Nationalism
(Theme Match): The war's outcomes initiated a political shift that eventually fueled nationalist ideologies.

4. British Consolidation at Fort William (Calcutta, 1740s)
Themes:
Colonial administration, trade consolidation, early imperial governance.

Relevant Lecture-Based Readings:
Barbara D. & Thomas R. Metcalf – Foundation of Colonial Rule
(Direct Match): Fort William was central to early British administration and control in Bengal.

Abhijit Banerjee & Lakshmi Iyer – Land Tenure Systems
(Direct Match): The administrative base established here became the platform for Permanent Settlement and other colonial reforms.

Benedict Anderson – Print Capitalism and Vernacularism
(Theme Match): Calcutta’s development supported early vernacular print cultures and political expression.

Niraja Gopal Jayal & Pratap Bhanu Mehta – Nationalism
(Theme Match): British expansion disrupted indigenous rule and created a political foundation for later nationalist resistance.

5. Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748)
Themes:
European diplomacy in India, Anglo-French rivalry, temporary peace and future conflicts.

Relevant Lecture-Based Readings:
Barbara D. & Thomas R. Metcalf – Conquest and Settlement
(Direct Match): The treaty preserved British gains and reflected colonial power dynamics.

Milton Singer – Sanskritization and Modernization
(Direct Match): Madras’ return under the treaty marked a resumption of colonial development.

Benedict Anderson – National Consciousness
(Theme Match): Diplomacy between European nations over Indian territories shows how colonial empires imagined and enforced control.

Niraja Gopal Jayal & Pratap Bhanu Mehta – Nationalism
(Theme Match): The treaty exemplifies foreign influence shaping Indian political structures and responses.
